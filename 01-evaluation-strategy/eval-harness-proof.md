## Version A, Concise, system prompt used

You are an executive briefing assistant. Summarize the supplied business update in exactly 3 bullets and fewer than 60 words total. Cover key results, risks, and actions where present. Use only source-supported information and preserve material qualifications.

## Version B, Narrative, system prompt used

You are a PR communications assistant. Summarize the supplied business update in about 100 words of narrative prose, with no bullets. Present wins first, then risks and any stated next steps. Use only source-supported information and preserve material qualifications.

## Eval setup, dataset name + judge model/family

- **Dataset:** [Ascend IQ noisy business updates v1](starter-dataset.json), 20 synthetic emails: 10 ordinary updates and 10 paired challenging variants.
- **Learner rationale:** Updates are often noisy.
- **Reference checklists:** Assistant-drafted results, risks, actions, and prohibited inferences for each row; learner review pending. These do not replace the learner's golden-set criteria.
- **Generator:** This Codex chat, OpenAI GPT family. Use the same model for both prompt versions.
- **Judge:** Independent judging omitted at the learner’s request to do the comparison in this chat. Assessment below is same-model self-review, not an independent judge result. Any optional LLM judge must be from a different model family, such as Anthropic Claude or Google Gemini, to reduce self-preference bias. This chat cannot serve as an independent different-family judge for its own outputs.
- **Execution status:** Dataset generated; one A/B comparison performed in this Codex chat on 12 October 2026 using the email supplied by the learner. Both outputs were generated by the same assistant with the respective prompts applied as task instructions, not isolated system-message runs. The 20-row dataset has not been evaluated.
- **Measurement scope:** This starter dataset supports factual-fidelity and robustness checks. A single chat comparison does not establish 95th-percentile latency; that requires repeated timed requests.

## Cold-start, the prompt you used to seed a starter dataset

Generate 20 synthetic business-update emails for Ascend IQ, which summarizes updates for busy executives. Create 10 ordinary emails and 10 challenging variants paired with those originals. Across the variants, add irrelevant detail, reorder sections, alter formatting, or introduce explicitly unresolved conflicting figures. Include some originals with no stated next action. For every row, provide an ID, pair ID, email text, essential source-supported results/risks/actions, claims a summary must not invent, and the challenge type. Preserve the original business facts except where a conflict is explicitly introduced. Do not generate summary outputs yet.

## Your definition of good vs bad (golden-set criteria) — the graded part, write your own

Criteria drafted by the assistant at the learner's explicit request, based on the selected executive user promise and trust metrics.

- **Good:** Every factual claim is supported by the input; numbers, uncertainty, and material qualifications are preserved. The summary includes all decision-critical results, risks, and explicitly stated actions, while excluding irrelevant detail and repetition. It follows its assigned format: Version A has exactly three bullets and fewer than 60 words; Version B has approximately 100 words of narrative, wins first and then risks, without bullets.
- **Bad:** Any invented or contradicted claim, changed number, unsupported action or owner, hidden material risk, or presentation of unresolved information as certain is grounds for rejection. Missing decision-critical information also fails. Noise, repetition, or format violations fail the usability criterion.
- **Decision rule:** First reject outputs that fail factual fidelity or essential coverage. Among passing outputs, prefer the one that communicates the necessary information with less reading effort for executives. Brevity never excuses omitting a material risk.
- **Limits:** Latency and robustness are not established by a single comparison. Latency requires repeated timed requests; robustness requires evaluating the paired ordinary and challenging inputs.
- **Assessment:** Same-model self-review in this chat only; no independent different-family judge score will be claimed.

## Comparison evidence — 12 October 2026

### Input

Provided by the learner as the walkthrough email; paragraph spacing normalized.

```text
Subject: Q4 Marketing Campaign Update

Hi team,

Social media engagement is up 25% month-over-month due to the new content calendar and paid ad experiments.

Newsletter open rate dropped from 42% to 35%; we'll test new subject lines next week.

The product video campaign is delayed until November, pending legal approval; creative assets are ready.

Please ensure all campaign tracking links are added by Friday.

Best,
Priya
```

### Version A output

- Social engagement rose 25% month-over-month through the new content calendar and paid ad experiments.
- Newsletter opens fell from 42% to 35%; test new subject lines next week.
- Product video delayed until November pending legal approval; creative assets ready. Add all campaign tracking links by Friday.

### Version B output

Social media engagement is up 25% month-over-month, driven by the new content calendar and paid ad experiments. Creative assets for the product video campaign are also ready. However, newsletter open rates have fallen from 42% to 35%, and new subject lines will be tested next week. The product video campaign is delayed until November while legal approval remains pending. The immediate tracking requirement is to ensure that all campaign tracking links are added by Friday. These are the results, outstanding approval dependency, and upcoming actions highlighted in Priya’s Q4 marketing update to the team.

### Checks and result

Word counts use whitespace-separated tokens, excluding bullet markers.

| Check | Version A | Version B |
|---|---|---|
| Word count | 45 | 94 |
| Assigned format | Pass: 3 bullets, fewer than 60 words | Pass: approximately 100 words, narrative, wins before risks |
| Source support (self-review) | No unsupported claims identified | No unsupported claims identified |
| Essential coverage (self-review) | Preserved | Preserved |
| Noise and repetition | Direct, compact | Final sentence repeats the purpose of the summary |

Both outputs retain the 25% month-over-month engagement increase and its stated causes; the 42% to 35% open-rate decline; next week's subject-line test; the November delay and pending legal approval; readiness of creative assets; and Friday's tracking-link deadline. Neither claims that legal approval has been granted or invents an action owner.

**Learner-confirmed winner: Version A.** It preserves the essential information with fewer words and no closing recap. Version B follows its length and tone instructions but adds reading effort; its final sentence is redundant under the drafted usability criterion. The learner confirmed Version A as the winner at the final checkpoint. The criteria and comparison rationale were drafted by the assistant at the learner’s request; this is not an independent judge score.

**Scope:** One input, two outputs, same-model self-review. This does not demonstrate robustness across the starter dataset, establish a population hallucination rate, or measure latency. Independent different-family judging was skipped.
